Ear training for harmony. A chord is an absolute object; the degree is a function of that chord given a tonal context. Both pages share the same harmonic engine and the same answer interface — the only thing that changes is where the sound comes from.

How to use Practice

Pick an exercise and a variant. Four of them are context-free — triads, seventh chords, intervals, upper degrees — so they just play a sound and ask what it was. Three place you somewhere: harmonic function needs a key established first, metre asks how the beat is grouped, modulation plays two passages and asks how the second relates to the first.

Where a key is established, the default plays a short piece in it rather than a bare cadence: that is how a key settles into your ear when you listen to music, with a melody moving inside it. The drone is harder still — it removes the crutch of short-term memory.

The answer has several fields rather than being multiple choice: degree, quality, seventh, extensions, inversion. Missing the seventh but naming the degree counts as half a success, and that is exactly the data the spaced repetition is built on — every field you get wrong becomes an item with its own accuracy, and later exercises are sampled towards your weak spots.

Shortcuts: Enter checks your answer, then moves to the next exercise. Space replays the chord.

Install it on your phone

This is a progressive web app, so it installs from the browser without an app store. On Android, Chrome offers “Install app”. On iPhone, tap Share then Add to Home Screen. You get an icon, full screen with no browser chrome, and — after the first session, once the piano samples are cached — it works with no connection at all.

Your answer history stays on the device that recorded it: it lives in that browser, and the installed app shares storage with the browser you installed it from.
